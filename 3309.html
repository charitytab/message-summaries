Jesus Christ The Same Yesterday, Today, And Forever

Minister: Brother William Branham
Date Preached: Wednesday, May 6, 1953 (evening)
Location: Jonesboro, Arkansas
Reference: 53-0506

Summary

The declaration of Hebrews 13:8 — that Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever — is not a comforting sentiment but a doctrinal statement with practical consequences: if He healed the sick, cast out demons, and raised the dead in the Gospels, then He is obligated by His own unchanging nature to do the same today, and the pool of Bethesda stands as proof that God has always had a resource for healing in every generation, and that His Son is that resource made flesh and dwelling among us still.

Scripture Foundation

  • Hebrews 13:8 — "Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever" — the theological foundation and title text of the message.
  • John 5:1–9 — The pool of Bethesda: the Angel troubling the water, the multitude of sick, halt, blind, and withered waiting for healing, and Jesus passing through and healing the man who had been infirm for thirty-eight years.
  • Numbers 21:8–9 — The brass serpent lifted up in the wilderness: one of the many Old Testament types showing that God has always provided a means of healing for His people.

Also referenced: 2 Kings 5:9–14 (Naaman and the Jordan River — God's healing through an unexpected, apparently insufficient method); John 3:14–15 (Jesus pointing back to the brass serpent as a type of Himself); Acts 10:38 ("God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him"); 2 Corinthians 5:19 ("God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself").

Main Points

  • Coming home — and God is already moving. Brother Branham arrived in Jonesboro unexpectedly, following a sudden divine impression to visit Brother Reed and the friends there. He described it as a homecoming: "Something said, 'Well, go down and see Brother Reed and all the folks down there.'" The spontaneous nature of the meeting itself was a testimony — God's movements are not always scheduled on a program.
  • God has never left His people without a resource for healing. From the brass serpent in the wilderness to the pool of Bethesda to the healing ministry of Jesus, Brother Branham traced an unbroken line of divine provision for sickness. "Since he fell from sin, or fell in sin back yonder, and sickness came into the world, God's had some way — a brass serpent, a pole, or a prophet, or something somewhere — that the people get to to be healed." The current ministry was not an innovation; it was another link in an unbroken chain.
  • The pool of Bethesda was God's provision for one dispensation — and Jesus fulfilled and exceeded it. The Angel troubling the water could only heal one person, the first one in — and only at unpredictable intervals. When Jesus walked by that same pool, the full power that had previously worked intermittently through an angelic disturbance was now present continuously, completely, and personally in Him. Jesus is not a lesser provision; He is the ultimate fulfillment of everything the pool pointed to.
  • Jesus did not heal everyone at Bethesda — but He healed the one He was sent to. Brother Branham addressed one of the most common challenges to divine healing: if Jesus was full of love, why did He walk past all those suffering people and heal only one? The answer is John 5:19 — "The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do." Jesus operated by vision and divine direction, healing the one He was specifically shown. This is the same principle operating in the healing campaigns today.
  • The unchanging Christ demands an updated faith. If Jesus is truly the same yesterday, today, and forever, then every argument against miracles today is ultimately an argument against the character of God. The God who healed at Bethesda, the God who raised Lazarus, the God who sent His Angel to trouble the water — He has not changed. The only thing that can prevent Him from working today is the same thing that prevented Him in Nazareth: unbelief.

Quote

"God was in Christ recognizing the world to Himself. Here is God Jehovah, dwelling in His Son, Christ Jesus, moving by this pool of water. And now, do you believe He was full of love?"

Brother Branham posed this question as the key to understanding why Jesus healed only one man at Bethesda: not a lack of love, and not a lack of power, but an absolute submission to the Father's direction. The same Emmanuel who walked past suffering people He loved and healed only the one He was sent to is the same Christ operating in the campaigns today — full of love, full of power, healing the ones He has been sent to, in the order the Father directs. This understanding protects the congregation from both false hope ("everyone will be healed if we pray enough") and false despair ("He passed me by").

Final Exhortation

Jesus Christ is the same tonight in Jonesboro, Arkansas, as He was at the pool of Bethesda. He has not grown weaker. He has not grown less compassionate. He is not rationing His healing power. The same God who sent an Angel to trouble the water so that one sick person could step in and be made whole has now sent His Son — and His Son is present in this meeting through the same Spirit that raised Him from the dead. Come to Him tonight as the man at the pool came: without argument, without condition, simply laying your case before the One whose name is the same today as it always has been. He will not pass you by.
