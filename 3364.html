Faith Is The Substance

Minister: Brother William Branham
Date Preached: Tuesday, May 8, 1951 (evening)
Location: Los Angeles, California
Reference: 51-0508

Summary

Faith is not a feeling, a formula, or an emotion — it is a substance, as real and tangible as anything the physical senses can detect, and when a believer lays hold of it according to Hebrews 11:1, they have already received what they are asking for, because faith is the evidence of things not yet seen by the eye but already possessed by the spirit.

Scripture Foundation

  • Hebrews 11:1 — "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen" — the primary text and the theological pillar of the entire message.
  • 2 Kings 20:1–7 — Hezekiah healed of boils: God sent the prophet Isaiah, announced healing, and used a fig poultice applied to the wound — an early example that God heals both supernaturally and through natural means, but that He alone is the Healer.
  • John 9:1–7 — Jesus making clay and anointing the blind man's eyes: using the very material of creation (earth) as an instrument of miraculous healing, underscoring that God is not limited by the method.

Also referenced: James 5:14–15 (anointing with oil and the prayer of faith); Mark 11:24 ("Whatsoever things ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive them, and ye shall have them"); Jonah 2 (Branham referenced Jonah being vomited up by the whale — the story of God's faithfulness even to a fleeing prophet); Acts 10:38 (God healing all who were oppressed by the devil).

Main Points

  • Faith is a real substance, not an emotional state. Brother Branham insisted that Hebrews 11:1 is not metaphorical: faith has substance — it is a spiritual reality that can be possessed, held, and acted upon. The person who truly has faith for healing already has the healing in their spirit; the physical manifestation in the body is only the outward confirmation of something already received. Faith does not wait for the evidence; faith is the evidence.
  • Medicine assists nature; God alone heals. Brother Branham delivered a clear and charitable treatment of medical science: no honest doctor claims that medicine heals — medicine assists the natural processes of the body that God designed. True healing — the overcoming of disease and the restoration of the body — belongs to God alone. This is not a condemnation of doctors; it is a clarification of what healing actually is and who the Healer is.
  • God has always used physical instruments without being limited by them. From Hezekiah's figs to the clay on the blind man's eyes, God has never been afraid of using natural materials as instruments of supernatural healing. The instrument is not the point; the obedience and faith are the point. Taking medicine, if it does not hinder faith, is no different from Isaiah applying the figs — what matters is the heart of the person receiving.
  • If medicine hinders faith, set it aside — but do not condemn those who use it. Brother Branham gave a nuanced and pastoral word: for the person whose faith is strong enough to receive directly from God, there is nothing wrong with setting medicine aside and trusting God fully. But for those whose faith needs the assistance of natural means, God has provided those too. No condemnation either way — only an invitation to press toward greater faith.
  • This night was ministers' night — a special gathering of clergy. Brother Branham noted that he had promised the ministers of the city a dedicated night, and this was it. He spoke to them as peers and co-laborers, addressing the theological foundations of divine healing that they would need to teach and sustain in their congregations after the campaign ended.

Quote

"Medicine does not heal. No doctor could say that medicine heals. Medicine only assists nature. God's the only Healer there is."

Brother Branham made this declaration not as a criticism of medicine but as a clarification of theology. He followed it immediately by affirming doctors and their necessity: "In the day that we're living in, where there's millions and billions of people, and an earth full of germs and diseases, what in the world could we do if we didn't have these things?" The point was not to remove medicine from the hands of the sick but to correctly identify who the Healer is — and to keep faith directed at the right Source.

Final Exhortation

Tonight, lay hold of the substance. Do not come to this altar looking for a feeling, looking for a sign, looking for something to happen in your body first — come having already received in your spirit what you are asking for. Hebrews 11:1 says faith is the substance of things hoped for: that means when you truly believe, you already have it. The manifestation in your body will follow the substance in your spirit. Whatever God has to work through — a prayer, an anointing, or simply the still small voice of His Word — He is the Healer, and He is faithful to fulfill what faith has laid hold of tonight.
