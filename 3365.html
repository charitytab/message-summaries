Expectations

Minister: Brother William Branham
Date Preached: Monday, May 7, 1951 (evening)
Location: Los Angeles, California
Reference: 51-0507

Summary

What you expect when you come to God is what you will receive — not because God plays favorites, but because expectation is simply faith openly expressed; and the mounting wave of testimonies from this Los Angeles campaign, including cancer healed, blind eyes opened, and the deaf hearing, stands as God's own testimony that He is willing, He is able, and He is meeting those who come expecting Him to act.

Scripture Foundation

  • Mark 10:46–52 — Blind Bartimaeus: his persistent, expectant cry — "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me" — refused to be silenced, and his expectation was fully met: "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."
  • Matthew 9:27–30 — The two blind men: "Believe ye that I am able to do this?" — Jesus grounding the miracle in their expectation and confession before touching their eyes.

Also referenced: Hebrews 11:1 (faith as the substance and evidence of things not yet seen — the theological root of all expectation); James 1:6–7 ("Let him ask in faith, nothing wavering: for he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed... let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord"); Matthew 7:7–8 ("Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you").

Main Points

  • Expectation opens the channel for God to move. Brother Branham opened this service surrounded by extraordinary testimonies: a woman healed of cancer in the worst stage — seen in a vision at his hotel room, identified by name and condition before she arrived — was now well and in the audience. X-rays confirming healings were being brought to the platform. These were not incidental; they were God's answer to a congregation that had come expecting Him.
  • The mail itself testified to the power of expectation. Letters had been pouring in all week from people who had attended the previous services, reporting healings that had happened days after the prayer line. Brother Branham said the mail was so heavy it would take a week to get through. He read from these letters to demonstrate: the people who had come with expectation had left carrying something — and it had manifested in their bodies.
  • Doubt closes the door that expectation opens. Brother Branham drew the contrast clearly: the person who comes with crossed arms and a "show me" attitude leaves the same way they came. The person who comes having already settled the question in their heart — "He is going to meet me tonight" — is the one who gives the testimony next week. The difference is not in God's willingness; it is in the expectation of the person approaching.
  • Great expectations produce great miracles. Reviewing the campaign to this point, Brother Branham noted that each night's results were exceeding the previous night's. He attributed this not to a crescendo in his own gift but to a crescendo in the faith of the people. As corporate expectation rose, so did the manifestations of healing. The greatest night was always the one where the people came most expectantly.
  • Expectation is not presumption — it is the posture of a child. There is nothing arrogant about coming to God expecting to receive; it is exactly what He invited. "Ask, and it shall be given" presupposes expectation. A child who runs to a loving father expecting to be held is not being presumptuous — they are simply responding to the nature of the relationship. Come to God as a child comes to a father.

Quote

"Cancer in the very worst stage — and that I had saw her in a vision, and was at my hotel room, and come, and spoke, and told who the lady was and what her conditions was. She was healed and normal and well now."

Brother Branham shared this testimony not to glorify the gift but to demonstrate what expectation produces. The woman had heard of the campaign and come expecting God to meet her. God had already seen her coming — the vision at the hotel room proved it. Her healing was not a surprise to Heaven; it was the fulfillment of something God had already prepared for someone who was on her way expecting to receive it.

Final Exhortation

Come tonight not hoping, not wishing, not wondering whether God might be willing — come expecting. You are not asking God to do something unusual or reluctant; you are asking Him to do what He has always done for those who believe. Every testimony you have heard this week is His personal invitation to you: "I did it for them; now come expecting Me to do it for you." Set your expectation like an anchor before you enter this prayer line, and do not move it no matter what you see or feel. Expect God — and He will not disappoint you.
