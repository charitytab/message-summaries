At Thy Word

Minister: Brother William Branham
Date Preached: Sunday, May 6, 1951 (evening)
Location: Los Angeles, California
Reference: 51-0506E

Summary

Simon Peter had fished all night and caught nothing — every circumstance, every natural indicator, said it was impossible — yet when Jesus spoke, Peter launched out "at Thy word," and the nets were filled to breaking; and that same principle of obedient faith that acts on the Word alone, regardless of what reason and experience say, is the key that unlocks divine healing for every person in the congregation tonight.

Scripture Foundation

  • Luke 5:1–11 — Simon Peter's "nevertheless at thy word": all night of fruitless effort, then one act of obedience at Christ's command producing abundance — the central text of the message.

Also referenced: Romans 4:19–21 (Abraham "staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief" — the Old Testament model of faith that acts on God's Word against all natural evidence); Mark 11:22–24 ("Have faith in God... whatsoever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive them, and ye shall have them"); Isaiah 53:5 ("By His stripes we are healed" — the redemptive basis for healing claimed in faith regardless of present symptoms).

Main Points

  • The "nevertheless" is the hinge of faith. Peter did not deny the facts: he had toiled all night and taken nothing — the circumstances were real, and his experience as a fisherman said the nets would come up empty again. But he added the word "nevertheless" and launched on the basis of Christ's command alone. That single word of obedient trust turned a night of failure into the greatest catch of his life.
  • Healing belongs to the second week. Brother Branham opened by celebrating the testimonies pouring in from the first week of the campaign — healings confirmed by letters, by people running up to the platform, by neighbors reporting miracles. He pointed to these not to boast but to build faith for what lay ahead: if God did these things last week, what will He do this week when faith is even higher?
  • Other ministers in the city are not competition — they are co-laborers. With two major campaigns running simultaneously in Los Angeles, Brother Branham explicitly prayed for the other meeting and refused any spirit of rivalry. One plants, another waters, but God gives the increase. He asked the congregation to pray for every work going on in the city.
  • Faith does not wait for circumstances to improve before obeying. The "at Thy word" of Luke 5 is the universal formula for receiving from God: hear what He has said, refuse to negotiate with circumstances, and act. Whether it is Abraham believing for a son at a hundred years old or a sick person rising from their bed, the principle is identical — the Word has been spoken, and faith acts on it.
  • Testimonies are the faith-fuel for the next miracle. Brother Branham consistently drew on fresh testimonies to build corporate faith. The lady who had just run up to place her testimony in his hand — "I was healed" — was not just a personal blessing; she was evidence for everyone present that God was actively working, and her testimony became an invitation for the next person to believe.

Quote

"Nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net."

Brother Branham held this as the posture every believer must take when approaching God for healing. Peter's "nevertheless" acknowledged the reality of his circumstances while refusing to let those circumstances override the authority of the spoken Word. It is the same "nevertheless" Abraham exercised, the same "nevertheless" that every person who has ever received a miracle from God has had to say in their heart before the miracle came.

Final Exhortation

You may have been praying a long time and seen nothing change. You may have tried every method, spoken to every minister, and your condition remains. Tonight, say your "nevertheless" — not to your feelings, not to your symptoms, not to your past disappointments, but to the Word of the living God. He has declared by His stripes you were healed. Launch out at His word. Let down the net of your faith one more time, not because the circumstances have changed, but because He has spoken — and His Word is more than enough.
