Believest Thou This

Minister: Brother William Branham
Date Preached: Sunday, May 6, 1951 (afternoon)
Location: Los Angeles, California
Reference: 51-0506A

Summary

When Jesus stood before Martha at the tomb of Lazarus and asked, "Believest thou this?" — He was not asking her to understand the mechanics of resurrection, but to make a simple, unconditional declaration of trust in His Person; and the same question He put to Martha He puts to every person sitting in this audience today: not "can you explain it?" but "do you believe it?"

Scripture Foundation

  • John 11:20–27 — Jesus' conversation with Martha: "I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: and whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?" — the central passage and question of the message.

Also referenced: John 11:32–44 (Mary's similar posture; Lazarus raised from the dead — the resolution that follows when faith answers the question of John 11:26 in the affirmative); Mark 9:23 ("If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth"); Matthew 9:28 ("Believe ye that I am able to do this?") — Jesus consistently asking the faith question before performing a miracle.

Main Points

  • The longest-attended were the first to receive. Brother Branham opened by observing something striking from the previous night's service: the cripples and wheelchair cases who had been in the prayer line the longest — dealt with more slowly, prayed over more thoroughly — were the very first ones to stand up and walk. The principle: those who tarried longest in faith received first. God rewards persistent, patient trust.
  • The question is not about ability — it is about belief. Jesus did not ask Martha, "Do you understand the resurrection?" He asked, "Believest thou this?" The challenge was not intellectual but volitional — a commitment of the will to trust His word over every circumstance. Brother Branham applied this directly: God is not asking anyone to understand how He heals; He is asking them to believe that He does.
  • Disclaiming the pulpit, embracing the altar. Brother Branham openly stated that he did not even claim to be a preacher — "Mine is praying for the sick." He was not there to deliver an eloquent sermon but to stand before the congregation as a man under commission, believing for miracles. His humility about his speaking ability was matched by his boldness about the gift he carried.
  • Fresh healings are proof that the question demands an answer. Cripples had walked out of wheelchairs the night before. The evidence of God's power was not theoretical — it was visible, documented, and sitting in the afternoon congregation. Brother Branham used these fresh testimonies not to boast but to sharpen the question: if that happened to them, why not to you? Do you believe He can do it for you too?
  • Faith is a declaration made before the evidence arrives. Martha's answer came before the stone was rolled away. She said, "Yea, Lord: I believe" — and only after that confession did Jesus give the command to open the tomb. The order matters: faith first, miracle second. Brother Branham consistently called the congregation to make the confession of belief before they came through the prayer line, not after.

Quote

"Believest thou this?"

Brother Branham returned repeatedly to this question from John 11:26 — Jesus' direct challenge to Martha before the resurrection of Lazarus. He used it as both a diagnostic and an invitation: a diagnostic to expose where doubt was still hiding, and an invitation to step into the kind of believing that sees the impossible become possible. Every person in the congregation was being asked the same question Martha was asked: not "can you explain God's power?" but "do you trust Him enough to say yes before the stone is rolled away?"

Final Exhortation

Jesus is standing before you this afternoon asking the same question He asked Martha: "Believest thou this?" Not "believest thou that I might do it," not "believest thou that I have done it for others" — but believest thou this, right now, for yourself? The cripples who walked out last night had to answer that question before they put their feet on the floor. The answer you give in your heart right now is the answer that determines what happens in the prayer line. Say it simply, say it from the heart, say it the way Martha said it: "Yea, Lord: I believe."
